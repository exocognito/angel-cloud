# Dogfooding run 2026-07-29 — findings

First run against [#5](https://github.com/exocognito/angel-cloud/issues/5), on
the merged preview/coordinate world. Sam ran it three ways in one sitting:
clicking round the dashboard cold, then reading the run sheet, then walking the
CLI path from an empty directory.

The raw notes and twenty screenshots stay on Sam's laptop, untracked, at
`.sam/dogfooding/2026-07-29-sam-m2.md`. They carry account names, consent
screens and live coordinates, so they do not belong in this repo. This file is
the record. Where it summarises a judgement rather than an observation, it says
so.

The run did not finish. Sam never held an Angel key and never called a
deployed Angel over MCP, so #5 stays open.

## Verdict

The engine is right and the outside of it is not. Sam's words: the core model
and philosophy are good; the exterior is convoluted and does not fit the paths
a real person would take. Almost every finding below is a seam between a
correct internal model and the way it reaches a person — vocabulary, page
shape, entry point, or a document that describes a different product from the
one that shipped.

## 2. The gap behind most of the rest: there is no ordinary account

Sam cannot start where a user starts. He enters through Cloudflare Access as
the operator, so every journey he tests begins three steps in, and he never
sees the empty dashboard a new person meets.

What he asked for, in his own shape:

- Sign in and out like anyone else. Magic link by email or SMS, expiring in
  minutes; passkeys optional on top. One email and/or one phone, at least one
  of them, because that is also the recovery path if a passkey is lost.
- Delete the account and start again from empty, on demand.
- An account page, and later billing and subscription management.
- **He does not want to build any of this.** He asked for research on the best
  options for auth, subscriptions and billing, and support and docs — what
  Cloudflare offers natively, and what the strongest third-party and open
  source choices are.

He also walked the flow he expects a new person to take: watch the demo or read
the home page → click through to a dashboard → land on a logged-in dashboard
showing placeholder data, essentially the demo site → hit the wall at "create an
Angel or change its tools" and make an account there.

The first thing a new account does is connect a provider, and that forks two
ways:

- **Bring your own OAuth client.** Creating the keys in the Google Cloud Console
  is, in his words, a nightmare — so this fork needs a numbered checklist with a
  link for every step. That is an actionable documentation deliverable, not an
  impression.
- **Use `angelmcp.ai`'s own client** — one "Sign in with Google" button, marked
  coming soon until Google verification lands.

And the payoff should be visible: once Google is connected, a wall of Google
product icons unlocks — Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Maps, Docs, Slides, Sheets,
Photos. That reward is the point, even for the person who only clicked one
button, because the work still felt like work.

This blocks honest dogfooding of everything upstream of an Angel, so it should
be sequenced before another run.

## 2a. The CLI path is too heavy for the person he is building for

Recorded separately because it cuts against the whole shape of the run.

Looking at the CLI journey he had just praised for its first-principles design,
he wrote: "for the typical user this is way way too much overhead. I can see
users just wanting to create angel in the browser/native ios app FOR SURE." The
browser would write the YAML and follow the right steps on their behalf. He
wants "as close to 1-click angel as possible for some users."

This sits under §10's page map rather than replacing it — the CLI still matters
for the terminal-first person. But a run that only proves the CLI path proves
the path he thinks most people will not take.

## 3. Angel detail: the page Sam specified

**Read §3a before acting on this.** What follows is specific enough to build
from, but Sam questioned the whole list immediately after writing it and told us
not to start now. Both halves are the finding.

He worked these out while clicking. Collapse five tabs into two.

- **Settings** is conceptually "turn off every tool toggle at once". It becomes
  a single select-all slider in the Angel heading, level with the per-tool
  toggles. The rest of the section goes.
- **Version History Remaining** moves to the top of Activity.
- **Agent Keys** moves to the top of Allowed Tools and stops being a tab. The
  MCP address *is* the Angel; it belongs on the front, and showing the keys
  there is fine.
- The top-right **production/preview toggle and LIVE indicator** are visually
  loud, never change, and do not earn the space. He proposed replacing them with
  a tab-level control: Activity already shows Production badges with a green
  dot, and clicking a badge switches the view to preview, so every tab would
  need at least one badge to carry it. He then changed his mind while writing
  point 4 below and lifted the control back up into the health bar. **The health
  bar is his final position**; the badge idea is recorded because it explains
  why the top-right toggle has to go either way.

What is left is Allowed Tools — really an Angel Detail page — and Activity.

Information architecture for that page, top to bottom:

1. Name.
2. Charter, verbatim from the YAML, with a link to view `ANGEL.yaml` — possibly
   as a tag rather than a link.
3. MCP address as the hero, with tabs for the other ways people start. "If I am
   in Claude Code, what do I copy from here?"
4. One canonical health bar. Today health is scattered across several
   indicators. Consolidated, it can be quieter and still sit prominently above
   the tools, and it should absorb the production version number (linking to
   version detail) and the environment toggle. Sam first objected to health
   appearing only as a warning; once enough is folded into it, the bar earns a
   permanent place.
5. The tool list, with the select-all pause at the top.
6. **Add tool** as the primary call to action, with one consistent flow every
   time.

The Angels list needs a **create Angel** button. It has none.

## 3a. What Sam said about his own list, immediately after writing it

He turned on it in the same sitting, and this governs how §3 is used.

- On the method: "I'm listing out functionality because I *feel* like I would
  make a compelling looking UI and people would understand the control." Writing
  it out made him doubt it.
- On the centrepiece: "it might be that we barely need any of these things. Like
  anchoring on pause/unpause looks very visual but makes no sense. I'm not
  signing in to urgently unpause a specific tool."
- On what the web UI actually is: a visual front end for the same jobs the CLI
  does, so "we should probably call it what it is, and ensure what is needed in
  the web UI version makes sense in the CLI and we codesign for both." No
  component gets designed for one surface alone.
- On sequencing, verbatim in intent: the UI is extremely functional for
  dogfooding right now; don't stop and redesign it. Note the areas to improve,
  defer most until a designer can take them, and fix only what blocks the next
  run.
- On the underlying principle, which is the real instruction: stop starting from
  "what is all the information we need to show the user" and start from "what
  does the user want to do here and how do we make it easy". Everything else can
  still be shown, by some secondary route that stays out of the way.

So §3 is a backlog with one designer-shaped item at the end of it, not a change
to schedule. Pull forward only what blocks dogfooding.

## 4. Vocabulary, and where it does not land

- Users do not hold **Provider** and **Connection** apart. Both read as one
  thing: an "account" or an "app" you enable and hand to your agent safely.
- **Arg guards** meant nothing to him at first sight; it read as a warning.
- Accounts render so small on the Angel page that he could not tell whether an
  account attaches at Angel level or somewhere else. His understanding, stated
  as a question and still unconfirmed here: an Angel may have several
  Connections, every allowed tool applies to all of them, and there is no way
  to scope different Connections to different tools inside one Angel.
- He wanted the interface types for Angel, Tool, Provider and Connection shown
  plainly, and what the data looks like when tools and accounts combine. That
  is the developer education piece he has not seen.
- The provider surface is shaped around one provider. "Connect a Provider App
  here — it's all so baked around Google. But what does it look like if we can
  connect google maps, google calendar, twitter, instagram, reddit,
  elevenlabs…". §11's data-room request is downstream of this.
- Nothing distinguishes a tool that is **available**, one that is **activated**,
  and one that is **blocked behind a credential you have not connected yet**.
  He expects to need all three states visible.

The four technical jobs, as he named them: create/edit/delete an Angel;
add/remove/edit a tool in an Angel; add/edit/delete Providers; add/remove/
refresh connected accounts.

## 5. Connecting an account

Walked end to end in the browser and it worked, with four frictions:

- The first attempt **500'd**. Re-authorising the same account then worked. The
  cause was never established. An expired Google external-testing grant fits —
  those die about every seven days and reauthorising is the known fix — but that
  is a hypothesis, not a diagnosis, and nobody looked at a log. What is certain
  is the defect either way: the error named no cause and no next step.
- Consent asked only for email-level scopes. He liked asking for little, but
  did not know when the rest would be requested.
- He expected one consent screen and got two.
- **He believed he had connected the account to the Angel.** He had not — the
  Connection was created attached to nothing, and joining it to an Angel is a
  separate act nobody told him about. This is the sharpest UX finding in the
  run.

Open design question he raised: how do we let someone pick broader tool access
and see the consent wall grow accordingly, rather than hiding it?

## 6. Docs and getting started

- `$EDITOR hosts.env` appeared with no account of where the file came from. It
  read as sketchy enough that he felt he had to go read the code. *Fixed in the
  run sheet during the session.*
- The run sheet opened with a wall of text, in his words LLM-ish, and the
  three-secrets table read as prerequisites rather than a summary of the whole
  process. *Fixed during the session: the page is now three tabs, and Getting
  Started stands alone.* The writing instruction behind it stands for everything
  we produce: say it at a ten-year-old's level first, then go back for the
  broader point and put it through Orwell's rules. And the product's
  chest-pounding details — the ones that announce how carefully something was
  built — land better said quietly, or not at all.
- `$DOCS/llms.txt` was written as a shell variable. He wanted links he could
  open in parallel tabs. He guessed two URLs, both failed — one redirects to the
  Access login, the other has no DNS record — gave up and read the file from the
  repo. **A getting-started link a person cannot copy and paste
  is a defect** — [#39](https://github.com/exocognito/angel-cloud/issues/39).
  *Fixed in the run sheet: every path in the reading table is now a real link.*
- Tag a session as a dogfooding session when it starts, so moments do not have
  to be tagged one at a time as they happen.
- Nothing is live on `angelmcp.ai`, so he fell back to source files —
  [#6](https://github.com/exocognito/angel-cloud/issues/6).

He handed `llms.txt` to a fresh Codex instance with instructions to read only
that file. It produced a correct eleven-step first-Angel path, including the
two owner-provisioned secrets and the one-time browser OAuth flow. So the
document works on an agent. It is people arriving at it who cannot get in.

## 7. Trust in what the build says

One finding and two open questions.

- **The finding.** A claim about 0.3.0's Gmail surface — 21 operations, no send,
  trash or delete — read to him as **suspiciously hard coded**. He wants golden
  end-to-end tests whose subject varies at random, so a test that has been
  overfitted to the wording of a spec fails visibly. His summary of the whole
  class: "we had terrible tracer bullets going through the slices."
- **Open question, asked as a question.** "How do I know when I uploaded to
  cloud that the cloud published exactly what I provided? Closing that loop
  seems pretty essential and I don't know if our system currently does, doesn't
  or isn't even aware it should." Partial answer, verified: `angel build`
  produces an artifact and a digest, publish sends both, and promotion carries
  the same digest forward, so a Version is addressed by content end to end.
  What is not established is whether anything lets *him* check that from
  outside — recompute the digest and compare it against what production reports.
  Record this as an unanswered question until someone traces it, not as a defect.
- Second open question: must an Angel served locally always be exercised without
  live credentials, or is that incidental?

## 8. Packaging and repo shape

- Why `angel-core` rather than `angel`? He recalls the answer: avoiding a
  rename while the scope is still `@smcllns`, with a move to the org later.
- **Why is `angel-core` a separate repo from `angel-cloud`?** He wants five
  whys on the security case, because on productivity grounds he would strongly
  prefer one repo — one codebase for the agents. He accepts that Angel Cloud
  needs its own Cloudflare account; he does not see why that implies a repo
  split.
- The install he wants to present is the `angel` CLI from `angelmcp.ai`:
  `curl angelmcp.ai/install.sh | sh`.
- Possible shape: one monorepo of `angel-core`, `angel-cloud`, `angel-catalog`
  — a community catalogue of shareable YAML, allowlist and charter, taken by
  pull request. He notes a git repo is a poor front door for volume; a database
  with git as the durable store, syncing hourly, fits better. The part he likes
  is that a plain YAML file is hard to write and easy to read, and can be
  verified independently at every build and deploy stage.

## 9. Preview and production

He never lost track of which environment he was in — the failure the run sheet
most expected did not happen.

But his model does not match what he was taught. He expected: push from the
CLI, land in preview, connect an account, query it privately, then make it
public when ready. And once it works in preview, he wants to stop. "Enable
production" sounds like a risk he did not ask to take.

He holds two things at once, and both are the finding:

- **For his own first Angel he wants preview.** "I want this to be in preview
  mode for my first one, I don't want this to be a public API with my account
  now."
- **As a default he wants the opposite**: production by default, preview
  optional — and he still wants to understand why the complexity exists at all.

His own fix is a line of copy, not a redesign: say it plainly in the
instructions and in the agent getting-started, framed as a choice — you want it
live now so we can test it, or quarantined in preview to test it privately.

Against the default question: production-by-default is already the decision.
PD 0003 point 1 makes production the default and preview opt-in; PD 0005 changed
only how preview binds Connections and leaves points 1 and 2 untouched. The
server deploys a published Version straight to production in one step, and the
CLI flip shipped in core 0.3.0 — `angel publish` now goes to production and
`--preview` is the opt-in. He had 0.1.0 installed, so he met the old two-step
behaviour, and PD 0003's own front matter still says the flip "waits on
`@smcllns/angel-core`". **The shipped design already agrees with him; the CLI he
got and the records he could have read both told him otherwise.**

His remaining question, which the decision records should answer in one sentence
and currently do not: what does preview buy me?

## 10. What Sam wants before more building

The clearest ask in the notes, and it is a process one.

He wants five things agreed and written as shorthand nested trees, so gaps and
misread intent are visible at a glance:

1. User goals and outcomes.
2. User guarantees.
3. A map of the CLI — **four menus and args**.
4. A map of mobile — **four tabs, one component at a time**.
5. A map of the web — **four pages, all components at once, dashboard style**.

Those three shapes are his, not paraphrase, and they are the skeleton the
section exists to hold. It is the same jobs to be done in all three; the format
is what changes.

He also named how the next run should be done: three ways, by the same person —
in a browser; asking an agent in the terminal from the very start; and using the
CLI for the occasional high-yield action he would do by hand. And flatly, after
this run: agree better dogfooding standards before running again.

Then build only the CLI and the web for now. The rule is pruning: anything
whose absence does not block progress comes out, because it can go back later.

His draft map of goals:

1. Create/delete/update an Angel.
2. Deploy — (a) create/delete/edit an Angel Cloud account, (b) self-host on
   your own Cloudflare account or from GitHub.
3. Create/delete/edit a Provider or app upstream.
4. Connect/remove/reauthorise an account or Connection.

His draft page map: Home and marketing; Angels list; Angel detail; Account.

He also wants the entry point treated as the thing that tells you who you are
designing for, because each one implies a different person:

- `brew install …` — wants to stay in the terminal.
- `curl angelmcp.ai/install | sh` — is in a terminal but probably should not be.
- "Read `https://angelmcp.ai/llms.txt` and tell me if this is safe to try, how
  you would demo it, what it costs, whether it is a scam" — wants to prompt the
  whole thing and touch a browser as little as possible.
- `git clone … && pnpm i && pnpm dev … wrangler deploy` — brings their own keys,
  hosts it themselves, and will open issues and pull requests.

Method he wants applied to all of it: agree the nested trees, then explore
component designs as carousels of wide variations, so opinions can be compared
without compromising between them prematurely.

Separately, he wants to settle on one spec-to-execution discipline and stop
improvising. Candidates he named: superpowers; Matt Pocock's wayfinder;
compound's plan-then-design; and the interview-to-goals-outcomes-evals shape.
He asked to compare all three or four and pick one for good.

## 14. Strategy notes

Not findings; recorded because they set the bar for what is worth building.

- Current scale — Sam, agents, Cloudflare, a few friends checking the reasoning,
  and the required approvals such as Google OAuth verification — is a fine level
  for now. Something works and is shipped. The question is who uses it.
- While Google verification is pending, add testers to the beta list by hand.
  A hundred people is enough to learn what share will pay, and that measurement
  is the goal.
- Sharing needs to be built into Angels more deeply than it is.
- He expects to hire a product designer, and expects a credential-holding
  platform to need serious security engineering. He asks, genuinely, whether
  running everything on Cloudflare with tight seams makes that tractable, or
  whether holding everyone's keys is reckless in the current climate.
- He is explicit that the disorganisation is upstream of the code: the
  specification and direction were patchy, and no amount of agent help covers
  for that. Hence §10.
